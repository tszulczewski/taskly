## Decision summary

The decision is which part to build first, and on 2026-10-16 the owner narrowed it again: version 1 is a simple prototype for the owner alone. A task has a description and an estimated cost range typed by the user; a task with subtasks takes its estimate from them instead, as the sum of their ranges (R12–R15, D22, decided 2026-10-27 after the prototype test). An exact price is typed once (R18, D24). A task also has important and urgent markers, assignment to several spaces, reordering, and completion. The only summary is future expenses, the sum of the main tasks' estimate ranges, without completed tasks (D01, D02, D04, D06, D07, D09, D13, D14, D17–D24; R09–R18). Real cost, payments, incurred expenses, expenses of completed tasks, effort, job order links, AI estimates, the shopping list and sharing move to version 2 (D05, D10, D11, D17, D18). A task without an estimate is left out of that total (R10).
The owner's account: they have many low-impact tasks (curtain rods, a decorative wall, pantry wall finish), forget some when choosing what to do next, and lose each task's cost in quick thinking (see Problems). Forgotten small purchases are a secondary problem (Later).
Monetization is not a goal (D03). Relayed by the owner: friends reportedly have the same problem and want to test (number unknown); the friend's account mentions no cost (A03). Testing by friends is placed after the first version because of D02.
Main uncertainty: whether seeing task costs and the future-expenses total actually changes the owner's ordering (A01); the 2026-10-27 test and the owner's own use say nothing about ordering yet. Second, whether typing estimates by hand is acceptable effort now that they add up from subtasks (A02): in the test the moved-in owner priced only subtasks and sometimes split jobs very finely, and the future owner typed exact prices twice.
Next: the specs follow D22–D24 (E01 and the future-expenses work); the owner uses the first part on their own task list and reviews which functions help or don't (Kill criteria); the owner decided research with other renovators happens now (Collection plan, no date set). A04, the ranking of the two problems, is still the owner's opinion; each problem has one recent episode; the grass case lists real losses without amounts.

## Scope

- **Now (version 1, single user):** a task list. A task has a description and an estimated cost as a min–max range typed by the user (R16, D14); it may have subtasks, each a small task with its own estimate; a task with subtasks has no estimate of its own but the sum of its subtasks' estimates (R12–R15, D22); an exact price is typed once (R18, D24); tasks can be ordered and reordered, and subtasks within their own task (D25); each task carries "important" and "urgent" markers (D07) and can be assigned to several rooms or spaces at once (D09), chosen from a ready list the owner can edit (D26); the owner can mark a task completed, which completes its subtasks (R09, D20); one summary, future expenses, is the sum of the main tasks' estimate ranges without completed tasks (R17, D19), with a list of what still has no estimate (D27) (D01, D02, D13).
- **Later (version 2):** real cost and payments, with the model still to be refined (D17); the incurred-expenses figure and the expenses of completed tasks, a single value that is not a range (D18); marking which job must come before which (D10, Q12); an effort level and assessment of the amount of work (D05); AI estimates of cost for subtasks and tasks (D04, D11); a phone-friendly quick shopping list; sharing and testing by friends.
- **Not doing:** see Non-goals.

## Places during creation (2026-11-13 clarification of D09, D21 and D26)

The owner requested choosing/adding places already while adding a task, rather than only after saving it, and subsequently authorized completing the documentation autonomously. This applies to both tasks and subtasks. The draft supports multiple existing places and creating a new catalog place; selection is optional, with no inheritance. Save commits the task and its assignments atomically. A failed save retains the draft; a successful save clears the selection for the next task. An explicitly created catalog place remains after cancelling the draft. These failure/cancel details are implementation defaults chosen for the authorized update, not additional interview observations.

Delivery: E02-S03 #19, AC11–AC18 and the Phase 2 follow-up in `2026-11-03-order-markers-and-spaces.md`; the base places implementation in PR #70 does not include this extension. This remains version 1 scope, not E04 Later.
