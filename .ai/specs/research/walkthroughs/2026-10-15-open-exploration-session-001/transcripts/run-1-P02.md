# Transcript run 1 P02

## Q1. Tell me about the last time you had to decide what to work on next in the house. What did you do, step by step?

**Passages attached:** "nie do końca wiem za co się zabrać, chcesz zrobić wszystko na raz i mieć skończone i finalnie nie zabierasz się za nic."

**Persona lines used:** Moved in already, did most finishing ourselves; some small and some bigger jobs remain; want to finish everything at once and end up starting nothing; tired after work; work on it only after the day job; money comes from what remains of the monthly salary.

**Fast reaction:** Ugh, that is exactly the problem. I look at everything that is left and I don't know where to grab it.

**Feeling:** Tiredness mixed with frustration, moderate. Trigger: the whole pile of remaining jobs at once, after work.

**Considered answer:** I come home tired, I look around and see all the jobs that are left. I want them all finished at once, so I think about the big ones, then the small ones, then what the money allows this month. Then I don't know where to begin and I end up doing nothing. When it does happen, it is whatever I have a bit of money for that month, and usually it is a small job.

**Adversary line:** And here is why I would still not use something like this: if it makes me enter a lot of information before it helps, I will be too tired after work to do it.

## Q2. What did that cost you — time, money, nerves? What did you try before?

**Passages attached:** "czasami zaczynamy jedno a okazuje się że jest za drogie i trzeba odłożyć, albo nie mamy materiałów i trzeba je kupić... albo nie mamy narzędzi i trzeba je kupić"; Excel not flexible, notes chaos, paper scattered.

**Persona lines used:** Nothing left over for finishing, step by step from the monthly salary, some months more some less; some jobs need saving up; tools tried: Excel (not flexible), notes (more chaos), paper (sheets lying around); searched for an app and found nothing with those functions and that view.

**Fast reaction:** Mostly nerves, and wasted starts. You begin something and it turns out it is too expensive, or you lack materials or a tool.

**Feeling:** Annoyance, moderate. Trigger: starting a job and having to stop halfway.

**Considered answer:** Money is the hard limit, so a job that turns out too expensive has to wait until I save up. Sometimes it is the materials or a tool I have to go and buy first, and that was not in my head when I started. I tried Excel, but it is not flexible. I tried notes, but that is more chaos. I tried paper, but the sheets are lying around and I don't know what is where. I looked for an app and found nothing with those functions and the view I want.

**Adversary line:** And here is why I would still not use something like this: I have already tried a few places for my list and each ended up as another mess, so a new one may just join the pile.

## Q3. Who else was involved, and who decided?

**Passages attached:** Cleaning up every evening because the spouse cooks in the kitchen and pets would make a mess.

**Persona lines used:** Dirty jobs in the kitchen must be cleaned up the same evening because of pets and a spouse who cooks; work only after the day job. (No basis: who else I plan with.)

**Fast reaction:** The spouse and the pets decide more than I'd like, because the kitchen has to be usable again by evening.

**Feeling:** Mild irritation, mild. Trigger: having to clean up everything the same evening.

**Considered answer:** A dirty job in the kitchen can't be left overnight, because the spouse cooks and the pets would make a mess. So a job like that has to fit into one evening after work, including the cleaning. Who else I plan the order with, I don't know, I can't say that honestly. It is mostly me deciding what to take on.

**Adversary line:** And here is why I would still not use something like this: I don't know whether it would fit how the two of us handle it, and I have no wish to set up something for more than one person.

## Q4. What would have to be true for you to say this problem is gone? What did you not care about that others might think you do?

**Passages attached:** "nie wiem ile to będzie kosztować i ile czasu zajmie... jaki nakład pracy jest i czy warto to robić przed czy po innym zadaniu"; the remaining jobs "nie przeszkadzają w życiu codziennym".

**Persona lines used:** Wants to know what to take on, what each job costs in money and in work, whether it is worth doing before or after another job; the remaining jobs don't disturb daily life; words "simple and quick", "intuitive".

**Fast reaction:** I would know for each job roughly what it costs and how much work it is, and which one goes before which.

**Feeling:** Hopeful, moderate. Trigger: imagining having a clear order instead of a pile.

**Considered answer:** The problem is gone when I can look and know what to take on now, what it costs in money and in work, and whether it is worth doing before or after another job. It has to be simple and quick, and intuitive. What I don't care about: the remaining jobs don't disturb daily life, so there is no rush and no deadline, and I don't need it to look pretty or to track every detail of the house.

**Adversary line:** And here is why I would still not use something like this: if I have to type the costs myself and it tells me nothing about how much work a job is, I'm still guessing the hard part.

## Q5. Last time you wrote down jobs and costs somewhere, what happened to that list?

**Passages attached:** "Czasami mniej więcej szacuję ile co wyjdzie, później zapominam i tak w kółko powtarzam szacunki i listę zadań zamiast to sobie gdzieś zapisać. Ale jak zapiszę to nie poprzesuwam tego wygodnie".

**Persona lines used:** Excel not flexible, notes chaos, paper scattered; want simple and quick. (No basis: whether I'd keep a list up to date for long.)

**Fast reaction:** It gets lost or it gets stale. And I redo the estimates in my head again and again.

**Feeling:** Mild embarrassment and weariness, moderate. Trigger: realising I keep repeating the same estimates.

**Considered answer:** Sometimes I roughly estimate what each job will cost, then I forget, and I repeat the estimates and the list again instead of writing it down somewhere. And when I do write it down, I can't move things around comfortably, so the list stops matching what I actually plan. How long I would keep a list up to date, I don't know, I haven't had one that lasted.

**Adversary line:** And here is why I would still not use something like this: I don't know that I would keep it current, and a list that is out of date is worse than none.

## Decision under pressure

**Pressure 1 (twenty minutes tonight, half-kept list already).** What I actually do: I open it, type in the two or three jobs I'm thinking about right now with a rough cost each, maybe mark one important, and look at what the total says. If I'm too tired I stop after that, and I don't touch the rest of the old list. I do not move the whole old list in. Difference from what I said earlier: I said I want everything finished at once, but in practice I would do a small slice only.

**Pressure 2 (nothing useful until about fifteen jobs with costs).** What I actually do: I type a few, see nothing, and close it. I go back to my Excel or my head. That is the complicated entry of lots of information I can't stand. Difference from what I said earlier: I said I want to know what to take on, but if I only get that after a lot of typing, I drop it before I get there.

**Pressure 3 (no time limit, quiet weekend morning).** What I actually do: I sit down with coffee and put in all the jobs, and try splitting a few of them into steps and assigning rooms. I would probably get through the list and see the total. Whether I would keep it up afterwards, I don't know. Difference from what I said earlier: on a weekend I do more than I claimed after work, but this is the one situation where I would, and it doesn't fix the evenings.
