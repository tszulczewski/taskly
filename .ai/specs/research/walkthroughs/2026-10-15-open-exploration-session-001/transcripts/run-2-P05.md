# Transcript run 2 P05

## Q1. Tell me about the last time you made an estimate of what a job would cost. What did you do, step by step?

**Question**: Tell me about the last time you made an estimate of what a job would cost. What did you do, step by step?

**Passages attached**: "Czasami mniej więcej szacuję ile co wyjdzie, później zapominam i tak w kółko powtarzam szacunki i listę zadań zamiast to sobie gdzieś zapisać."

**Persona lines used**: I live in my house now; I roughly estimate costs, then forget, and repeat the estimates and the list of jobs; I am weary because the same work repeats.

**Fast reaction**: Honestly, I just add it up in my head, roughly. And then it is gone.

**Feeling**: Weary, moderate. Trigger: having to do the same counting again.

**Considered answer**: I think about the job, I roughly work out what it might come to, and I carry on. A few days later I do not remember the figure, so I go through the list of jobs and the guesses again from the start. I could not tell you one particular time in detail, it is the same loop each time. I never put it anywhere, that is the problem.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: if it makes me fill in too much before it gives me anything, I will go back to doing it in my head.

## Q2. What did that cost you — time, money, nerves?

**Question**: What did that cost you — time, money, nerves?

**Passages attached**: "zaczynamy jedno a okazuje się że jest za drogie i trzeba odłożyć, albo nie mamy materiałów i trzeba je kupić... narzędzi... a to też kosztuje".

**Persona lines used**: I start a job and find it too expensive and have to put it off; sometimes I lack materials or tools and have to buy them, which costs too.

**Fast reaction**: All three, but mostly nerves and money.

**Feeling**: Frustration, moderate. Trigger: starting a job and then having to stop.

**Considered answer**: You start one thing and it turns out too expensive, so it has to wait. Or you find you do not have the materials, or the tools, and you have to buy them, and that costs as well. Then the job stands half done. The time goes on going over the same list again.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: it would not stop the surprise of a tool I forgot I needed, because I type the numbers myself and I would forget that too.

## Q3. Who else was involved, and who decided?

**Question**: Who else was involved, and who decided?

**Passages attached**: "nie mamy też już pieniędzy na wykończenie i wykończenie robimy stopniowo z pozostałej kwoty z wypłaty" (we).

**Persona lines used**: This topic matters a lot to me; (the passage uses "we").

**Fast reaction**: We decide together, and it comes down to what is left of the pay.

**Feeling**: Mild resignation. Trigger: money running short for the finishing.

**Considered answer**: It is a "we" thing. There is no money left for the finishing now, so we do the finishing bit by bit from what remains of the pay. So what we do next depends on what is left that month. I have no more detail than that about who says the last word.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: if the other person will not look at it, I am again the only one who keeps it up.

## Q4. What would have to be true for you to say the problem is gone? What did you not care about that others might think you do?

**Question**: What would have to be true for you to say the problem is gone? What did you not care about that others might think you do?

**Passages attached**: "nie wiemy co zrobić najpierw, a co później, jaki nakład pracy jest i czy warto to robić przed czy po innym zadaniu".

**Persona lines used**: I want to know what to do first, how much work each job is, and whether it is worth doing before or after another job; I would know it works if I could rearrange the list easily.

**Fast reaction**: I would know what to do first, and I could move things around easily.

**Feeling**: Hope, moderate. Trigger: picturing the list simply rearranging.

**Considered answer**: The problem is gone when I know what goes first and what later, how much work each job is, and whether it is worth doing before or after another one. And I can rearrange the list without a fuss. What I do not care about is a perfect, detailed plan. I would not want to fill in a million pieces of information. Simple and quick, intuitive, that is what counts.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: "simple" is easy to promise, and the moment it asks for a lot of detail I am out.

## Q5. Last time you wrote the list somewhere and tried to move things around, what happened?

**Question**: Last time you wrote the list somewhere and tried to move things around, what happened?

**Passages attached**: "Ale jak zapiszę to nie poprzesuwam tego wygodnie, excel nie jest taki elastyczny, notatki tym bardziej, będzie większy chaos, a na papierze to też do kitu".

**Persona lines used**: Excel is not that flexible; notes make more chaos; paper sheets lie around. No basis: what I do with a list once it is written.

**Fast reaction**: It was awkward. I could not move things around comfortably.

**Feeling**: Annoyance, moderate. Trigger: the tool getting in the way of rearranging.

**Considered answer**: Excel is not that flexible. Notes are worse, they make more chaos. Paper sheets just lie around and it is no good. So writing it down did not help me shift things. What I then did with the list afterwards, I don't know, I haven't kept track of that.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: it would be one more place for a list to sit, and I would stop opening it like the others.

## Decision under pressure

**Pressure 1: twenty minutes tonight, after work, a half-kept list already.**
What I do: I would probably open it, put in the few jobs on my mind and move them about, and not type many costs. If it is quick I may keep going; if it asks for a lot, I close it. Differs from earlier: I said I want simple and quick, and here I would just do the easy part and skip the numbers, which is the very thing that I keep forgetting.

**Pressure 2: nothing useful until about fifteen jobs with costs typed in.**
What I do: I most likely stop partway. Fifteen jobs with costs is a million pieces of information for me. I might do a few and then leave it, and it joins the other lists. Differs from earlier: I said this topic matters a lot, yet I would not push through it. The wish is real, the patience for typing is not.

**Pressure 3: no time limit, quiet weekend morning.**
What I do: I sit down with it and enter the jobs, probably with rough costs, and play with the order. That is when I could really see what goes first. Differs from earlier: I would do more than I said I would with a quick evening, so it depends on the day. I still don't know whether I would come back to it later.
