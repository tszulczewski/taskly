## Q1. The last time you had to figure out the order of the work

**Question**: Tell me about the last time you had to figure out the order of the work. What did you do, step by step?

**Passages attached**: advice that the kitchen should be designed earlier to place lights, sockets, plumbing; "o widzisz a ja bym zrobił już wszystko a później dopiero kuchnię i znowu bym musiał kuć ściany".

**Persona lines used**: about to take over a flat in developer state, first property; list of main works with rough costs; acquaintance said design the kitchen early; had planned everything else first and kitchen later; surprised that order matters.

**Fast reaction**: Honestly, I didn't figure it out, someone told me. I had my list and I was going to do the kitchen last.

**Feeling**: Relief, moderate, triggered by hearing I would not have to chisel the walls twice. Under it, unease, mild to moderate, because I wonder what else I have in the wrong order.

**Considered answer**: I have a list of the main works with rough costs. I had it in my head that you do everything and then the kitchen. An acquaintance told me to design the kitchen early so lights, sockets and plumbing get placed once. "O widzisz, a ja bym zrobił już wszystko a później dopiero kuchnię i znowu bym musiał kuć ściany." Since then I'm not sure about the rest of the order. I haven't started any work yet. I don't have a method for it, I just ask.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: it does not know what blocks what, so it would not have told me about the kitchen. That is exactly the thing I got wrong.

## Q2. What did that cost you, and what did you try before

**Question**: What did that cost you, time, money, worry? What did you try before?

**Passages attached**: "Chciałem zrobić to w excelu ale zrobił się chaos... jakbym rozbił sobie to na części pierwsze mógłbym to lepiej oszacować i rozplanować".

**Persona lines used**: not started work; works full time operating machinery; tried Excel and it became chaos; wants to split big jobs into smaller parts to estimate and plan better.

**Fast reaction**: Nothing yet, luckily, I found out before I started. The worry is big, though.

**Feeling**: Worry, moderate to strong, triggered by the amount of money involved and this being my first flat.

**Considered answer**: I wanted to do it in Excel but it turned into chaos. If I broke each job into its parts I could estimate and plan it better. That is what I'm missing, the parts. A big line like "bathroom" tells me nothing. I don't know what a job consists of. I haven't lost money on this yet, so I can't give you a figure.

**Adversary line**: And here is why I would still not use something like this: if I have to break everything into parts myself in a phone app, it may become the same chaos as Excel, just smaller.
